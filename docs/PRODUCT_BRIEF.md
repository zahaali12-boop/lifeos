# What "best in the market" means (non-negotiable)

1. **The books are always right.** Every financial event creates a balanced double-entry journal through one central posting engine. Posted records are immutable: corrections are reversals or adjustments, never edits or deletes. Subledgers (AR, AP, inventory, fixed assets, bank) reconcile to their GL control accounts at every moment, and automated tests prove it.
2. **Real time.** Stock, costs, balances and reports reflect a transaction the moment it's saved. No overnight posting runs.
3. **Every number explains itself.** Any figure on any screen or report drills down to the journal lines and source documents behind it. Prices, costs, taxes and blocked transactions come with a "why" breakdown.
4. **Configurable without code.** Admins set up custom fields, approval workflows, numbering series, print layouts, roles, dimensions and reports in the UI.
5. **Multi-everything from day one:** companies, branches, warehouses, currencies, languages (with full right-to-left support), units of measure, price lists and tax regimes. Retrofitting these later is how older ERPs became slow and brittle, so they belong in the foundations.
6. **Fast and pleasant.** Keyboard-first, command palette, global search across every record, saved views, bulk actions, WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility. It should feel like the best modern SaaS products, not a legacy ERP. Target p95 under 300 ms for everyday screens and API calls.
7. **Open and auditable.** Everything the UI can do, the documented API can do; webhooks, bulk import/export, no data lock-in. Least-privilege access down to company, branch, warehouse, document type and field, plus a tamper-evident audit log of who changed what, when, and from what to what.
8. **Fast to implement.** Guided setup, industry templates and validated migration tools, so a new customer goes live in days, not months.

# Functional scope

## Sales (order to cash)
- Customer 360: contacts, addresses, pipeline/opportunities, activities and full transaction history on one screen.
- Quotations → sales orders → pick/pack/ship → invoices → receipts, with partial deliveries and invoicing, backorders, drop-shipping and cancellation of remaining quantities. The document chain is linked both ways.
- Pricing engine: price lists by customer, group, currency, date range and quantity breaks; line and document discounts; promotions and bundles. Deterministic, explainable results.
- Credit control: credit limits and overdue blocks, holds released through approval, every override logged.
- Returns (RMA) and credit notes that restore stock at the correct cost and reverse revenue, tax and COGS.
- Sales reps and commissions, recurring invoices, customer statements, aging and collections, available-to-promise and reservations.

## Purchasing (procure to pay)
- Requisitions → RFQs with supplier quote comparison → purchase orders → goods receipts → supplier invoices → payments. Supplier returns and debit notes, blanket agreements, supplier advances.
- Three-way match (PO, receipt, invoice) with configurable price and quantity tolerances; mismatches block payment and route to approval.
- Landed costs (freight, customs, duties, insurance, other charges), including invoices that arrive after the goods, allocated by value, weight, volume or quantity and split between stock still on hand and stock already sold (COGS).
- Approval rules by amount, department, category and supplier; supplier lead times, price history and performance scoring.

## Inventory and warehousing
- Multiple warehouses with bins/locations, transfers with in-transit stock, reservations, and consignment stock in both directions.
- An append-only stock ledger is the single source of truth; on-hand balances are derived from it.
- Costing: FIFO, moving weighted average, and standard cost with variances, configurable per company or item category. Backdated transactions trigger correct cost recalculation, with the GL adjusted and the reason traceable.
- Lot/batch and serial tracking with expiry dates, FEFO and full forward/backward traceability.
- Exact unit-of-measure conversions (buy by the carton, stock by the piece, sell by the dozen), variants, kits and simple assemblies.
- Reorder points, min/max, safety stock and purchase suggestions. Cycle counts and full stock takes with count freeze, variance approval and GL posting with reason codes.
- Barcode/QR scanning in a mobile web app for receiving, picking, transfers and counts.
- Inventory valuation equals the inventory GL balance at any date, to the smallest currency unit.

## Accounting and finance
- Hierarchical chart of accounts with templates; analytic dimensions (cost center, department, branch, project/job, plus admin-defined ones) on every journal line.
- General ledger, recurring and reversing journals, accruals and prepayments.
- AR and AP with payment allocation (partial, overpayment, on account, customer deposits), netting and write-offs.
- Multi-currency: transaction, functional and reporting currencies; dated exchange rates; realized FX on settlement; month-end revaluation of open items with automatic reversal.
- Tax engine: VAT/GST/sales tax, inclusive and exclusive pricing, withholding tax, exemptions, reverse charge, tax reports, and pluggable adapters for country e-invoicing and clearance schemes (e.g., Peppol, ZATCA, CFDI).
- Bank and cash: multiple accounts, statement import (CSV, OFX, MT940, CAMT.053), rule-based auto-reconciliation, petty cash, expense claims, and post-dated cheques received and issued, including bounced cheques.
- Fixed assets: register, depreciation methods, revaluation, disposal.
- Budgets by account and dimension, with versions and budget vs actual.
- Multi-company with intercompany transactions and consolidation with eliminations.
- Period management: soft and hard locks, close checklist, year-end close to retained earnings, controlled reopening.
- Financial statements (trial balance, P&L, balance sheet, cash flow) with comparatives. IFRS-aligned by default; local statutory charts and parallel books for local GAAP when required.

## Management and platform
- Users, roles and granular permissions (module, action, document type, field, and record scope by company/branch/warehouse), with segregation-of-duties checks.
- Workflow and approval engine: multi-level rules, thresholds, delegation, escalation, mobile approvals.
- Numbering series per company, branch, year and document type, gapless where the law requires it.
- Custom fields on any entity; comments, attachments and activity history on any document; notifications.
- Executive dashboards: cash position, AR/AP aging, DSO/DPO, inventory turns and aging, gross margin by product/customer/rep/branch, budget vs actual.
- Localization: languages, full RTL, date and number formats, time zones, non-calendar fiscal years.
- Onboarding: guided setup, industry templates, and a validated import toolkit for master data, opening balances and open documents.
- If SaaS: tenant provisioning, subscription plans and feature flags.

## Reporting and analytics (our strongest selling point)
- A semantic data layer across all modules, so business users build reports from friendly field names: filters, grouping, pivots, calculated fields and charts, saved, shared and parameterized.
- Every cell drills down to the underlying lines and source documents.
- Financial report designer: user-defined row/column layouts for P&L, balance sheet and management reports by any dimension, with budget vs actual, prior-period and year-over-year columns.
- Designable print templates for invoices, orders and statements, including bilingual layouts.
- Scheduled email delivery; export to Excel, PDF and CSV.
- Row-level security: every report and export shows only the data the user's role allows.
- Reports run on read-optimized models (materialized aggregates or a columnar store, your call) so heavy reporting never slows daily work. Target: interactive pivots over 10M+ transaction lines in a few seconds.

## AI layer (later milestone, designed for from the start)
- Ask-your-data: plain-language questions become reports the user can inspect, edit and save.
- Anomaly detection (duplicate supplier invoices, unusual margins, suspicious journal entries), demand forecasting, smarter reorder suggestions, and supplier-invoice capture from PDFs and photos.
- Guardrails: AI respects the user's permissions, shows the query behind every answer, and never posts to the ledger without human approval.

## Out of scope for v1 (but architected to plug in later)
Full manufacturing/MRP, HR and payroll, point of sale, e-commerce connectors.

# Architecture guidance

Propose the final architecture and justify each major decision in an Architecture Decision Record (ADR). Start from these defaults unless you have a strong reason to change them:
- A modular monolith with strictly enforced module boundaries, communicating through explicit interfaces and domain events. Extract services only when a measured need appears.
- PostgreSQL. Money and quantities as exact decimals, never floating point; every amount stored with its currency; rounding rules explicit and centralized.
- One posting engine turns business documents into journal entries through configurable posting rules (account determination). No module writes to the GL directly.
- Append-only ledgers for accounting and stock; derived balances can be rebuilt from them deterministically.
- Concurrency safety: no overselling or duplicate numbers under simultaneous users, idempotent APIs, a transactional outbox for events and webhooks, background workers for heavy jobs.
- If SaaS, a deliberately chosen multi-tenancy model with isolation proven by tests.
- API-first with versioned OpenAPI docs. A typed web front end with a real design system, virtualized data grids, and i18n and RTL from the first screen.
- Stack: if I haven't specified one, choose it and justify it. Requirements: strong typing, first-class decimal arithmetic, mature migrations, excellent testing tools, a large hiring pool.
- Security built in (SSO/OIDC, MFA, encryption in transit and at rest, secrets outside the code, dependency scanning, OWASP ASVS Level 2) and observability (structured logs, metrics, tracing).
- One-command local setup, and CI running linting, type checks and the full test suite on every change.

# The hard scenarios (each one becomes an automated end-to-end test)

This is where most ERPs break. Ours must get every one right, and prove it:
1. A backdated purchase receipt arrives after the goods were sold: costs recalculate, COGS and inventory adjust in the GL, and the audit trail shows why.
2. Freight and customs invoices for an import arrive three weeks after half the shipment was sold: landed cost splits correctly between remaining stock and COGS.
3. A EUR invoice in a USD-functional company is paid in two instalments at different rates with bank fees deducted: realized FX and fees post correctly; the open balance is revalued at month-end and auto-reversed next month.
4. Two users sell the last unit at the same moment: exactly one succeeds (or the configured negative-stock policy applies), and no balance is corrupted.
5. A supplier invoice exceeds PO price tolerance: payment is blocked and routed for approval.
6. A customer over their credit limit places an order: it's held, released by an authorized approver, and the override is logged.
7. Someone tries to post into a closed period: blocked; the correction posts into the open period, referencing the original.
8. An intercompany sale between two group companies in different currencies: mirrored entries in both, eliminated on consolidation.
9. An item bought in cartons of 24, stocked in pieces and sold by the dozen: exact conversions, no rounding drift over thousands of transactions.
10. A post-dated cheque is received, deposited at maturity, and bounces: customer balance restored, bank charges posted, history preserved.
11. A stock count runs while the warehouse keeps operating: movements during the count are handled correctly against the freeze snapshot.
12. A customer returns an item from a lot that has since been recalled: stock status, credit note and traceability are all correct.
13. A serial-numbered item is sold, returned, repaired and resold: its full history is on one screen.
14. Quantity-break price, customer-specific price, a promotion and a foreign currency on one line: a deterministic result with a clear "why this price" breakdown.
15. At any historical date: AR aging equals the AR control account, AP aging equals the AP control account, inventory valuation equals the inventory GL, and the trial balance balances.
16. Year-end close moves P&L to retained earnings; reopening requires permission and is fully audited.
17. A gross-margin report by customer × product category × sales rep × branch, for any date range, returns in seconds on millions of lines and drills down to invoice lines.
18. If SaaS: no request, report, export, search or background job can ever touch another tenant's data.
