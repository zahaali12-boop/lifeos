## Known gaps and interim pieces (explicit, per the working rules)

- **Payables and banking (4.7) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-113): netting with receivables waits for the receivables module (5.7); a payment goes out of an account in the payment currency or in the company's currency (a third currency waits for the cross-currency transfer mechanics of 6.1); a cheque is a payment method only (cheque books, post-dated cheques and their events are 6.2); bank statements, reconciliation, transfers, charges and interest as documents of their own are 6.1, so a bank account's balance today is the sum of its payment movements; write-offs and revaluation of open items are 6.5 (the settlement kinds exist, nothing creates them yet); a proposal is executed as draft payments per supplier to be posted one by one (no bank file or batch approval yet); withholding at payment is computed on the gross amount of each line by the supplier's code (no threshold logic at payment, the invoice-time threshold stays as built in 4.4); company bank account numbers are stored in clear and masked on display (the partner bank accounts of 4.1 are encrypted; the same protection moves here with 6.1); the bank invariant compares each account's control lines with its transactions only where a line references a transaction, because the demo books and manual journals still move bank accounts without one until 6.1 makes every bank movement a transaction (the payables invariant, by contrast, sums every AP line, which is why the demo seed now opens and settles real open items behind its supplier journals).
- **Returns and debit notes (4.6) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-112): supplier advances (payment on account) moved from 4.6 to 4.7, where bank and cash accounts arrive: an advance is a bank-side posting and its open item settles invoices the same way a debit note does (`ap_settlements` already carries the `advance_application` kind). A return does not touch the order's quantities (the order was fulfilled; a replacement is a new order or a new receipt against the open line), and it does not lower what the receipt can still be invoiced for beyond the returned quantity; the supplier's credit comes as a debit note. A debit note credits returned goods at the returned cost and books what the supplier credits above or below it as purchase price variance, in the note's currency at the note's rate; where the note is in a foreign currency, the GRNI credit in the company's currency can differ from the return's cost by a rounding unit of that currency, which then stays under the return's reference (the invariant accounts for it) until 4.7's revaluation and write-off tools clear such residues. Credit application is same-currency only (cross-currency application waits for the payment engine of 4.7, which brings the rate handling); a settlement is not reversed on its own yet — reverse the debit note after unapplying, which 4.7 adds together with payment reversal.
- **Landed costs (4.5) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-111): a document lands on receipt lines of one company and is allocated in the company's currency at the posting-date rate; charges entered straight from a supplier invoice without an estimate (POSTING_RULES "Cr AP when entered from the charge invoice") go through a document first (draft, post, then the charge line settles it), which keeps one allocation path; the sold portion is what the costing engine's adjustment run re-applied to consumption (for average costing that follows the engine's re-application, not a per-layer split); weight and volume come from the item master (per base unit), not from the receipt; a settled document is reversed by reversing the charge invoice first; the allocation report is the document view (a company-wide report by period joins the reporting work of M8).
- **Supplier invoices (4.4) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-110): tolerance rules are the supplier account's effective price and quantity tolerances (no separate rule table by category yet; `pur_tolerance_rules` of the domain model waits for a need); tax lines are zero until the tax engine (M5) prices them and the input-tax line joins the journal; prepayment schedules (deferrals) and capitalisation to assets (M7) are not on the invoice yet; a matched line is invoiced in the order's currency; a receipt line invoiced by two invoices at different prices carries the later price on its stock entries; dimension sets on invoice lines are stored but not yet passed to the journal; the payables view is the open-item list (aging, proposals and payments are 4.7); the invoice's approval workflow and its blocks are separate definitions (on-submit and on-block), as the engine defines them.
- **Goods receipts (4.3) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-109): services and expense items are not received on a goods receipt (they are settled by the supplier invoice, 4.4); a receipt is reversed as a whole, partial returns are the supplier-return document of 4.6; quality hold on receipt is not a separate state yet (the stock engine's quality-hold bins from 3.5 can be named on the line); mobile receiving posts at once and needs a connection (it does not join the offline queue of 3.8); bins, lots and serials are typed on the line rather than scanned from the receiving screen's list; drop-ship receipts (POSTING_RULES) wait for the sales module.
- **Purchasing (4.2) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-108): purchase-order tax is 0 until the tax engine of M5 (the columns and totals exist); commitments are recorded on approval and released on cancel, but budget control (`budget_exceeded` blocks and overrides) waits for M6, as does consuming commitments at invoice; the purchase-order email carries a bilingual HTML body, the PDF attachment comes with the print pipeline (5.9); supplier quotes are recorded by the buyer (the supplier portal is M8); quote comparison converts at today's spot rate with no landed-cost model beyond freight and other charges; requisition lines without an item are ordered by hand from the order screen; the web line editor takes item codes (with suggestions) and unit codes; receipts, invoices, landed costs and returns are 4.3–4.6, so an order's received and invoiced quantities stay zero for now.
- **Supplier master (4.1) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-107): the customer side of the partner (customer accounts, credit limits, sales reps, price lists) arrives with M5; withholding codes live in `ptr_wht_codes` until the tax engine (M5) applies them at invoice or payment, when they become `tax_codes` of kind `withholding`; `tax_group_id` on the supplier account is a plain uuid until then; supplier portal users, relationships (affiliate, parent) beyond the parent link, opportunities and pipeline stages (CRM) are M5/M8; the item master's supplier references (`itm_item_suppliers.partner_id`) are still plain uuids and the demo seed does not yet create partners (both with the purchasing seed of 4.9); no CSV import/export of partners yet (the import toolkit of M8); performance scores are 4.8; the web screens create and edit but do not delete contacts, addresses, bank accounts or registrations (the API does).
- **Workflow core (4.0) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-106): approvers are named members or a role; the manager chain and dynamic owners (cost-centre owner, category owner, account manager) wait for positions and those masters; `on_post_attempt` and `on_field_change` are accepted triggers with no caller yet (documents run `on_submit`; the posting-time re-run and field-change re-approval arrive with purchasing documents); the reapproval policy is stored, not yet enforced (no subject re-evaluates after an approval); `require_step_up` is stored, not enforced (step-up authentication lands with the identity pass of M4/M5); email approval links, one-click approve, bulk approve and the mobile inbox are 8.1; stock adjustments are the only real subject so far (journals keep their company-setting approval until they move to the engine), and counts, transfers and assemblies raise no blocks yet (negative stock, price floor and credit limit blocks come with the documents that hit them); the web definitions editor writes conditions as text with a checker (the visual rule builder is 8.1) and picks escalation by role only (members through the API).

- **Item references to modules that do not exist yet are plain uuids** (ASSUMPTIONS A-096): supplier `partner_id` (partners, M4/M5), `item_tax_group_id` (tax, M5), `default_bin_id` in warehouse settings (warehouses themselves are real foreign keys since 3.2), `image_attachment_id` (an attachment uploaded through `/collaboration/attachments` with `entityType: item`; existence is not checked). Items have no costing override of their own: the category's applies, then the company's method.
- **M3 web pass boundaries**: the item screens edit units, barcodes, suppliers, per-warehouse planning and per-company costing settings since the post-M4 routine pass, and, since the same pass, bills of material, variants (with the attributes they combine), substitutes and the item picture; reservations and inbound cost adjustments have no screen by design (A-116); standard costs, revaluations and the per-entry cost explanation have screens since the post-M4 routine pass; document lines name items by code with suggestions by code or name; transfers ship in part and receive with shortages since the post-M4 routine pass; the ⌘K search covers companies, items and suppliers (documents by number are not searchable yet).
- **Demo seed v4 (4.9) boundaries** (ASSUMPTIONS A-115): the months before the current one hold purchase orders only (closed, not posted), because the demo books hard-close them and stock goes live on the first of the current month; posted receipts, invoices, landed costs, returns and payments exist in the current month only; since the post-M4 routine pass each company also has a requisition turned into an order received and invoiced in two parts, an active blanket agreement with its first release, an invoice blocked by a price variance and an approved requisition waiting to be ordered, but there are no foreign-currency purchases in the demo yet; the seed adds under a minute to the roughly six of v3.
- **Demo seed v3 (3.9) boundaries**: the item master and the opening stock only; no movements after the opening date (receipts, shipments, transfers, counts and their documents arrive with the M4–M5 seeds), no BOMs, substitutes or item images; suppliers are the party references of v2 (partner masters land in M4); the seed takes about six minutes because every item goes through the item service and every unit through the posting engine, which posts one journal per item scope and costs about 25 ms per stock line in round trips (a bulk path would be faster but would bypass the rules; batching the engine's per-scope journals into one per document is a candidate improvement for M4, where receipts carry many lines).
- **Mobile scanning (3.8) boundaries**: the scanner is the web app installed as a PWA, not a native app (no background sync, no push; the queue replays while the page is open); camera decoding is best-effort (BarcodeDetector on Chromium/Android, ZXing elsewhere, and the typed input that hardware scanners use anyway) and is not covered by the browser journey, which drives the typed input; a capture sets the counted quantity of its line (the count API's semantics), it does not add to a previous capture of the same line; a code unknown to the device while offline is sent as typed and fails in the queue if the API cannot resolve it; the transfer flow is one-step between warehouses (no bins, lots or serials on transfer lines yet, no receiving flow); GS1 application identifiers (lot and expiry inside one barcode) are not parsed.
- **Replenishment (3.7) boundaries**: the planner is a reorder-point/min-max planner, not a forecast (demand forecasting is the intelligence milestone); incoming supply comes from approved purchase orders, and suggestions become draft purchase orders from the replenishment screen since the post-M4 routine pass (A-134); suggestions are per item and warehouse (no transfer suggestions between warehouses, no supplier minimum order quantities or pack rounding); the run is daily by schedule and on demand, without a per-item planning calendar.
- **Counts (3.6) boundaries**: count sheets are entered through the API or the mobile scanner (3.8); the variance value shown at review is an estimate at the item's average cost (the posted value comes from the costing engine); a count has one approver step, not the workflow engine (M6); a blocking count blocks the whole warehouse (or its bins) rather than only the counted items; the snapshot takes what the balances say and does not detect stock that was never booked anywhere but is found on the shelf except through a "found" entry.
- **Tracking (3.5) boundaries**: a lot's `consumed` status is not set automatically when its stock reaches zero (it stays `active` with nothing on hand); serial statuses `sold`, `returned`, `consigned` and the counterparties come from the `partnerId` the calling document passes (the sales and purchasing modules will, M4/M5; the tests pass ids); a serial in repair keeps its stock row on hold but there is no repair document (costs of a repair are a positive adjustment); FEFO is a suggestion and a refusal when no lot is named, not an automatic pick (the sales module will pick, M5); warranty dates and lot/serial custom fields are stored and editable but not used by any rule yet; the recall impact list names partner ids (partner masters land in M4/M5).
- **Stock documents (3.4) boundaries**: adjustment approval is a single approver (the company setting), not the workflow engine of ADR-0020 (which lands in M6 and will take these documents over); an assembly consumes its components at the posting date in one go (no work-in-progress, no partial builds, no disassembly); the assembly variance account is used only under standard cost; a shortage on a transfer receipt is written off at the transit warehouse's cost (there is no claim on the carrier or supplier yet). Under standard cost a revaluation writes the pool row (the standard itself is not changed by an NRV write-down).
- **Costing engine boundaries (3.3)**: the expected/actual split is kept per receipt, not carried through applications (COGS taken from an uninvoiced layer is reported as actual; the valuation's "expected" column is the uninvoiced receipts still on the books). A sale return is valued at the original shipment's cost only when the caller names the shipment entry (`appliesToSleId`; the sales module will, M5), otherwise at the current cost. Consigned stock is not valued; drop-ship pass-through waits for M4/M5. A re-application beyond the threshold defers the whole valuation of the triggering posting (its own value entries included) to the job, so the posting result shows a zero cost until the run completes. A run that cascades through transfers posts one journal per scope walk and GL date, so one trigger document can have several journals. The permutation property test covers company scope; warehouse scope is exercised by the transfer path only. The valuation report and the cost history are API-only until the M3 web pass. Lot and serial ids on a line are accepted as plain uuids and are validated (existence, expiry, FEFO) only when 3.5 lands. The `approve` negative-stock policy is treated as `block` until the approval workflow exists. In-transit quantity is derived from open transfers (no balance row for transit beyond the in-transit warehouse's own on hand). No `WAREHOUSE` dimension value is created for a warehouse yet (the dimension lands with the costing postings). No warehouse or stock screens in the web app yet: the module is driven through the API until the M3 web pass.
- **Item units are editable without a stock guard**: changing or removing a unit (or the base unit) is refused only by the item's own rules (base locked while other units exist, units used by bills of material kept); the guard against changing factors once stock has moved in that unit is still open (it belongs with the costing engine, 3.3, where a factor change would revalue layers). No item screens in the web app yet: the item master is driven through the API and CSV until the M3 web pass.
- **Audit anchoring defaults to the file store.** `Quicker:Audit:Anchoring:Store=object_lock` writes retained objects through `IObjectStorage` (S3 object lock in compliance mode with the `s3` provider; the `filesystem` provider only refuses through its own API, so on a single node the file store's hash-linked lines remain the tamper-evident choice). Anchoring and verification now run daily as the platform jobs `audit.anchor_all` (02:00 UTC) and `audit.verify_all` (03:00 UTC; the job fails, and is visible as such, when any chain is broken). Verification and export from the API still run synchronously inside a request (page size 2000, export capped at 50 000 rows).
- **Audit retention** (dropping partitions older than the configured period, default 10 years) is not implemented; nothing deletes audit data yet.
- **Email is SMTP only.** `Quicker:Email:Provider=smtp` sends through any relay (Mailpit locally); the default `capture` keeps messages in memory and logs them. Provider APIs (SES, SendGrid, Postmark), bounce handling and per-tenant sender identities are not implemented; the identity module's invitation and reset emails still go straight to `IEmailSender`, not through the notification log.
- **Attachments are not scanned.** Uploads are limited by size and stored by hash, but there is no malware scan; objects orphaned by a failed commit after upload are not swept yet. Files, like comments, follow the record's read permission (see the next entry).
- **Security keys are a second factor, not a password replacement yet**: they are registered on My account and used after the password at sign-in, and the browser journey runs a real WebAuthn ceremony (Chromium's virtual authenticator, attestation `none`) against the server; step-up takes a key too; passwordless sign-in with a passkey and attestation formats other than `none` (the server asks for none, so keys do not prove their make) are not built. A workspace that requires two-step verification enrols people without it through an authenticator app at sign-in; a key can be added afterwards.
- **Paging and filtering are applied where lists are unbounded** (notifications, activities, webhook deliveries, audit events) and the filter language to companies; other lists (companies, branches, roles, series, jobs, schedules) return whole arrays until they can grow, and each new list endpoint adopts `Page<T>` and a `FilterSpec` as it lands. `or` is not in the filter language (use `in`). Version ETags with `If-Match` are on companies; other resources have body-hash ETags (304 only) until they expose `updatedAt`.
- **Subledger effects of a reversal are not mirrored yet**: the engine reverses the journal (lines, balances, links); the modules that own open items, value entries, asset and bank transactions (M3–M6) reverse their rows in the same transaction when they arrive, reacting to the `accounting.journal.posted` event or calling the engine themselves. Auto-reversal on `auto_reverse_on` is executed by the daily routine (`accounting.daily_routines`, 00:30 UTC) or on demand through `POST /accounting/routines/run`.
- **Posting-rule keys for tax codes, warehouses, bank accounts, asset categories and charge types are plain uuids** until their modules exist (M3–M6 add the foreign keys); item and partner posting groups and branches are validated today.
- **The Iraq statutory chart is group level and provisional** (nine groups of the Unified Accounting System; ASSUMPTIONS Q9): the mapping mechanism, the seeded reference table and the template mapping are complete, the detailed statutory codes below the groups are added once an Iraqi accountant validates them. Statutory reports themselves belong to M2.5/M6.
- **Accounting screens**: statutory mappings, chart CSV import/export, journal import and journal attachments (`entityType: manual_journal`, tenant-wide permissions, editable after posting) have screens since the post-M4 routine pass, as do posting profiles and rules, recurring templates and deferral schedules; each run of the daily routines is logged per company (see the routine run log entry). The journal editor takes account codes and amounts, and each line's details take its dimensions, supplier or customer and description (A-125); a bank control line's item is typed as a reference. The account editor refuses changes of meaning on accounts that already have lines (A-130). The trial balance screen takes one dimension filter at a time (the API takes any number).
- **Stock postings do not store their source document's number** on the journal entry (the costing engine passes none, and it is left as it is in this pass): the entry, the journal browser and the ledger read the number from numbering when showing them, but the stored column stays empty, so a direct SQL or report over `gl_journal_entries` sees no number for those entries until the costing engine passes it. Document search lists numbers only; drafts (which have none yet) are found on their lists. Branch and warehouse scopes on a grant are not applied to search results (an allocation does not know its warehouse), matching the document lists, which do not apply them either.
- **Posting windows are per role or for everyone, not per user or per module**; a window applies to every module's postings through the engine (GL today) and owners are held to the company window like anyone else. The close checklist and year-end close of ADR-0026 are M6 work.
- **The invariant harness reads the ledger tables and the numbering tables directly** (read-only SQL, as the reporting layer does) rather than through each module's contract; the audit chain goes through `IAuditChainVerifier`. Its job is exercised through the handler with a platform job context, not through the worker loop (like the accounting daily routine).
- **The daily routine runs once a day at 00:30 UTC for every tenant**; a company whose day starts later posts its reversals and recurring journals with the date they carry, not the wall-clock date. A per-tenant schedule can be set in `ops.schedules` (payload `{asOf}` accepted). The job is exercised through the routine service and the endpoint; no test drives it through the worker loop yet.
- **Deferral schedules planned before a company's fiscal years exist fall back to calendar month ends** and keep `fiscal_period_id` empty; they post into whatever period covers the date at posting time. Recurring templates count runs from the cron only (no `every N periods` shorthand).
- **An account with postings keeps its meaning** (A-130): its type, whether it holds postings, its subledger reconciliation and any currency or company restriction its lines would break are refused on edit and on chart import; the balance moves to a new account by journal instead. Moving it under another header of the same type, renaming and deactivating stay open.
- **The demo tenant is purged physically on reseed**, audit chain included, through the owner connection in maintenance mode; that path exists only for the `demo` slug and the seeder, and no API or job deletes a tenant (tenant offboarding with retention is a later platform slice). Demo passwords are shared and printed by the command; a deployment that exposes the demo publicly should reseed it on a schedule.
- **Demo seed v2 stops at the general ledger**: its customers and suppliers exist only as subledger references on the AR and AP control accounts (fixed ids, bilingual names in the journal descriptions), and its sales, purchases, collections and payments are manual journals, not source documents; partner masters, items, warehouses and the documents that post through posting rules arrive with the seed versions of M3–M6, when the same references become real open items. Company, branch, chart, account and journal ids are assigned by the services, so they change on reseed while tenant, user, membership and party ids stay fixed. The seed posts as the system actor inside one transaction and takes about six minutes with the v3 stock (progress lines mark the phases); a reseed while the API is serving the demo tenant makes in-flight requests fail until it commits.
- **The API contract may still break before the first release tag**: the gate compares against `main`, and this branch's own history shows one intentional break (three lists became page envelopes). From the first tagged release on, A-082 applies without exception.
- **Every operation in the contract describes its success response** (a schema for bodies, `string`/`binary` for files, or a bodiless 202, 204 or redirect), and the contract check fails on any that does not, so the generated client stays typed end to end. The contract is one document for the whole API, not per tenant: custom fields are described by `GET /collaboration/custom-fields/{entityType}/schema`.
- **Logs reach the collector through stdout, not OTLP**: Serilog writes JSON to the console for the platform's log shipper (Loki in the local profile reads container output); the OTLP log signal is not wired (`Serilog.Sinks.OpenTelemetry` when a deployment needs it). Runtime and process metrics (GC, thread pool) are not exported yet; log levels are set at start, not adjustable at runtime; the per-tenant "system health" admin page (ADR-0025) reads the same gauges but is not built yet.
- **Rate limits are in-process per node** (sliding windows in memory; the Development settings allow 300 sign-in calls a minute per address instead of 20, because the browser journeys sign many people in from one address): a multi-node deployment multiplies the budget by the node count until a shared store (Redis) backs the limiter (1.11 hardening).
- **Custom fields are hosted by companies and the six purchasing documents** (requisitions, orders, receipts, invoices, landed costs, returns), with forms for all of them; items, partners, stock documents and journals do not carry them yet. Each module registers its table when it adds them; the OpenAPI document is not rewritten per tenant, clients read `GET /collaboration/custom-fields/{entityType}/schema` instead. Index DDL runs without `CONCURRENTLY` (fine while tables are small; the online variant is a follow-up before large tenants).
- **Comments, files, timelines and links follow the record's read permission and its company** (A-133, A-136): a member needs the record's module permission (for example `purchasing.order.read`) as well as the collaboration permission, held for the record's company when that permission is limited to some companies; items and partners are shared by every company of a workspace, so their discussion follows the permission alone. Warehouse and branch scopes do not narrow a record's discussion yet (a role limited to one warehouse sees the discussion of every stock document of its companies). A member can still be mentioned on a record they cannot read (they are notified, and the record stays closed to them). Saved views are per list, not per record.
- **Central Bank of Iraq rate adapter is not implemented.** The roadmap lists ECB, Open Exchange Rates and a CBI adapter; the first two ship (parsers covered by tests, import covered end to end against a local stub). The CBI page has no stable machine-readable feed I could verify from this environment, so the adapter is deferred rather than shipped untested; Iraq's official and market rates are entered manually or through a rate type an integration fills. Neither live feed has been called from this environment (no egress); the first production import should be watched.
- **Scheduled rate import** is available as the tenant job `organization.rates.import` (`PUT /api/v1/platform/schedules {code, jobType, cron, payload:{provider, rateType}}`); no tenant has one by default.
- **Fiscal year closing** (`status = closed`, closing entry, reopening a year) belongs to the Closing module (ADR-0026 year-end); today years are `future` or `open`, and period states are the only lock.
- **Company `chart_id` and `posting_profile_id`** are plain nullable columns until Accounting (M2) creates the tables they reference.
- **Numbering has no shipped series.** Series are admin data created through the API; each document module (M2 onwards) registers the default series its document types need when it arrives, so a fresh tenant has no series until then. Document types are free codes until the document registry exists.
- **Numbering roles.** No shipped role template grants `numbering.*` yet; the owner/admin wildcard covers it. Role templates gain numbering grants with the accounting roles in M2. The same holds for `platform.*` (jobs and schedules).
- **Outbox archive is a delete.** Published messages and their inbox rows older than 30 days are deleted by `ops.outbox_archive`; the archive partition of ADR-0010 arrives when reporting needs event history.
- **A dead letter blocks its aggregate** until an operator retries it or discards it with a reason (strict per-aggregate order). Operators are made with the migrator (`operator --email`), not through the API; operator actions on the outbox are kept on the message and in the log, not in a workspace's audit chain (A-128).
- **Embedded worker is off by default** (`Quicker:Worker:Embedded=false`); `make up` runs the separate `worker` service. The worker image is not built in CI (only the solution is built and tested there).
- **Job progress is polled**, not pushed: the server-sent events channel for progress arrives with the web shell (1.10).
- **Idempotency-Key is optional on the server**: the web client sends one on every write since the post-M4 routine pass (and resends a write once under the same key when the network loses its answer), but integrations and scripts may not yet, so `Quicker:Api:Idempotency:Required` stays `false` by default (A-135). Responses above 1 MB, files, and 401/403 refusals are not stored (a replay re-executes); a replay carries the status, body and content type but not other headers (`Location`, `ETag`).
- **Webhook filters are applied** since the post-M4 routine pass: `aggregateType`, or any top-level field of the event payload (such as `companyId`), all of them must match (text comparison, letter case ignored); nested fields and lists of values are not filterable yet. The event catalogue section of the OpenAPI document (AsyncAPI-style) is part of 1.9.
- **Outbound requests to tenant-chosen addresses reach the public internet only** (webhook receivers, single sign-on providers): internal networks an operator wants reachable are listed in `Quicker:Outbound:AllowedPrivateNetworks`. These clients connect directly, never through an HTTP proxy, so a deployment whose egress must go through a proxy cannot deliver webhooks yet (A-127). The exchange-rate providers and the breached-password check call fixed, operator-configured hosts and are not restricted.
- **The web shell covers the admin screens of 1.4–1.8 at v1 depth**: calendars, units, settings, SoD rules and exceptions, API keys, SSO connections and the sign-in policy have screens since the post-M4 routine pass; comments, files and history have a tab on purchase orders, supplier invoices, requests for quotation, landed costs, suppliers, items and journals, and Discussion and History toggles under the details of requisitions, receipts, returns, blanket agreements, supplier payments, payment proposals, stock adjustments, transfers, counts, assemblies and revaluations, since the post-M4 routine pass; every audit event opens in full. Assignments limited to several companies, branches and warehouses, the network allow-list, SSO group-to-role maps and revoking an SoD exception have screens too (A-126). The daily accounting routines keep a run log per company (Recurring & deferrals → Run history). Permission descriptions come from the code-defined catalogue in English (the keys and every label around them are bilingual). Members who sign in only through SSO have no password to step up with; they sign in again (step-up through the identity provider is a later slice). Forms are controlled React state, not TanStack Form yet; the grid exports what it shows (A-129) but has no inline editing, grouping or pinning yet (data grid v2 with the first document lists). The company form sends the posting policies it does not show (defaults on create, unchanged on edit) because the contract marks them required. The 100k-row grid performance budget is met by virtualization but not yet measured in CI.
- **The shell is responsive** (sidebar drawer under 1024px, 44px touch targets); the mobile scanning app of 3.8 runs in it (see its boundaries above).

## How to resume in a new session

1. Read `CLAUDE.md`, then this file, then `docs/ROADMAP.md` for the current milestone and slice.
2. Re-read `docs/PRODUCT_BRIEF.md` once per session; skim `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md` for anything marked `changed`.
3. Consult the ADR that governs the area you are about to touch before writing code.
4. Work in small commits; update this file and the relevant docs in the same commit as the code.
